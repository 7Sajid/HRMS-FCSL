**FIRST CAPITAL SECURITIES LIMITED**

# **HR Management System**

*A plain-language breakdown  ·  Version 1.0*

This document explains what the HR software will do, who will use it, what each person sees on their screen, and how work moves from one person to the next.

It is written so that **anyone in the company can read it**.

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **WORDS USED IN THIS DOCUMENT****RM** — Relationship Manager. Staff who deal with clients, also called AR (Authorised Representative). **RM certificate** — The BSEC licence an RM must hold to deal with clients. It has an issue date and an expiry date, and must be renewed.**TWS** — Trader Work Station. A trading terminal ID issued for the exchange and held by one named person.**Panel** — What a person sees after logging in. ## **1. What this software is for**

Today, most of FCSL's staff information lives in three places: paper files in a cabinet, spreadsheets on somebody's computer, and people's memory. That works until it doesn't.

### **The six problems it is meant to fix**

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| **Problem today** | **What the system does about it** |
| **Nobody knows when a licence is about to expire** | An RM certificate has an expiry date. If it lapses, that person legally cannot deal with clients. Right now this is tracked by whoever remembers. The system warns **four months before** every expiry, automatically, to the RM, their manager and HR. |
| **Employee documents are scattered and incomplete** | NID copies, certificates, appointment letters, bank details. When BSEC or an auditor asks for a staff file, someone has to hunt. The system keeps one complete folder per person and shows at a glance who is missing what. |
| **Leave runs on paper and verbal approval** | An employee asks a manager, the manager says yes, and HR may or may not hear about it. Every request becomes written, timed and traceable, and the employee sees the decision without walking to anyone's desk. |
| **Branch attendance arrives late and in different shapes** | Each branch sends its monthly attendance in its own format. Every branch manager gets the same sheet, the same deadline, and one button to submit it. |
| **Nobody knows the real headcount** | When people leave, the list is not reliably updated. The system marks leavers on a date, so the count of active employees, RMs and managers is always correct and can be produced instantly. |
| **There is no record of who approved what** | For a BSEC-regulated brokerage this is the serious one. Every approval, rejection and document view is recorded with the person's name and the exact time, and can never be edited afterwards. |

## **3. The locked door**

If you remember one thing from this document, remember this. It is the idea the entire system is built on, and it applies to **every single person** — the newest junior clerk and the HR Head alike.

|  |
| :-: |
| **THE RULE IN ONE SENTENCE****When your account is first created you can do exactly one thing: upload your documents. Everything else stays locked until the person above you checks those documents and approves them.** |

  

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **STAGE 1 — LOCKED** | **THE CHECK** | **STAGE 2 — OPEN** |
| The account exists and the person can log in. Their panel contains one item: **Upload my documents**. Everything else is greyed out and cannot be opened. | The level above opens each file, fills in anything missing, and presses **Approve**. If something is wrong they press **Send back** instead, with a written reason. Only the boxes named in the reason reopen. | The full panel for that person's role appears: personal information, documents, emergency contacts, leave and attendance, calendar and notes — plus extra pages if they are a manager or HR. |

### **Who opens whose door**

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **This person's documents…** | **…are approved by** | **What happens on approval** |
| Employee / RM | **HR Head or HR Executive** | The employee ID is issued and the four employee pages open. |
| Manager / Department Head | **HR Head or HR Executive** | The four employee pages open, plus the approval page for their team. |
| HR Executive | **HR Head** | The four employee pages open, plus the HR working pages. |
| HR Head | **Super Admin** | Everything opens, including branches, compliance, exits and reports. |
| Super Admin | **Created at installation** | See the box below. |

  

### **Why the locked door is worth the inconvenience**

It would be easier to let everyone in immediately and chase the paperwork later. That is how most offices end up with half-empty staff files. Putting the wall at the start means nobody is ever in the system without a complete file — the file *is* the entry ticket; HR checks documents once, at the moment the person is most motivated to hand them over; and when an auditor asks for staff records, there is no such thing as an incomplete one.

|  |
| :-: |
| **THE COST OF THIS DESIGN, STATED HONESTLY (Future Plan)**A new joiner cannot use the system on day one. They log in, see an upload screen, and wait for HR. If HR is slow, the person sits blocked. The system therefore shows HR a queue of people waiting, sorted by how long they have been waiting, and sends a reminder if anyone has been waiting more than two working days. |

  

## **4. A new employee's first week**

This is the whole onboarding process told as a story.

#### **Step 1 — Before day one, HR creates the account**

The interview is finished and the person has accepted. An HR Executive opens the system and creates an account using **three pieces of information only**: name, email address and mobile number. Nothing else is needed yet. HR also chooses what kind of user this will be — employee, RM or manager — because that decides what will eventually unlock and which documents will be demanded.

#### **Step 2 — The password is handed over by a person**

The system generates a temporary password and HR passes it on by hand, by phone, or in the joining meeting. The password works once. The first thing the system asks for after that first login is a new password of the person's own choosing.

|  |
| :-: |
| **TWO SITUATIONS WORTH PLANNING FOR****The joiner never uploads anything.** The account simply sits at Stage 1. It cannot be used for anything, so it is harmless — but it should not sit there for ever. After thirty days with nothing uploaded, HR is told and can chase the person or deactivate the account.**The temporary password is never used.** It expires after seven days and HR generates a new one. An unused temporary password that stays valid for months is a way into the system that nobody is watching. |

  
  
  
  
  
  

#### **Step 3 — First login, the upload screen**

The new joiner logs in and sees the locked panel from Section 3. One screen, one job: upload documents. Each required document is a labelled box. As each file goes in, the box turns green. A progress line at the top reads, for example, *"6 of 9 required documents uploaded"*. The Submit button stays grey until every required box is green.

| NID | **Required** | **Required** | **Required** | Both sides. The system reads this one — see step 4. |

#### **Step 4 — The system reads the documents and fills the form**

When the NID and certificates go in, the system reads them and fills in the full name, father's name, mother's name, date of birth, gender and nationality by itself.

|  |

| **READING, BUT NEVER TRUSTING BLINDLY**Every field the system fills this way is marked with a small amber dot meaning **"read from a document, not yet confirmed"**. HR sees the original scan on one side of the screen and the filled field on the other, and confirms or corrects each one. The amber dot only turns green when a human has agreed with it. **Nothing enters an employee's permanent record on the word of a machine alone.** |

Religion, marital status and anything the documents do not contain stay blank for HR to fill in.

  

#### **Step 5 — HR reviews, the most important half-hour**

The joiner presses Submit and the file lands in HR's queue. An HR Executive opens it and works down a checklist: is each document readable, is it the right document, do the auto-read fields match the scan, are the bank details complete. Then one of two things happens.

Everything already accepted stays accepted, so nothing is uploaded twice. |

The employee logs in and the pages described in Section 5 are there. Their file is complete, their ID exists, they are attached to a branch and a manager, and they can apply for leave from that day forward. The whole sequence, if HR is prompt, takes about two days.

## **5. What each person sees when they log in**

### **5.1   Employee and RM**

**Page 1 — My personal information.** A read-only summary: employee ID, full name, father's and mother's name, date of birth, photograph, gender, nationality, religion, marital status. The employee looks at it; HR edits it. A **Request a correction** button sends a short note to HR rather than changing anything directly.

**Page 2 — My documents.** Every file uploaded, with the date and whether HR accepted it.  So an employee can add a training certificate, a confirmation letter or clearance documents at any time, but cannot replace or delete a document HR has already accepted. Each new upload goes to HR as a small approval of its own.

**Page 3 — Emergency contacts.** Both contacts, displayed. **The employee may edit this page**, and the change goes to HR as a one-click approval. The previous version is kept.  Explained in Section 10.

**Page 4 — Leave and monthly attendance.** Three things on one screen: apply for leave; see every application this month with its current position in the approval chain; and see the month's attendance, which appears only **after HR publishes it**. Before that it reads "Not yet published".

**Always available — calendar and notes.** Every panel carries a calendar showing public holidays, the Friday–Saturday weekly off and the person's own approved leave, plus a private notes area that nobody else can read. *(FCSL, 10 September 2026)* Each note is written for a day picked on the calendar — a meeting in three days is noted on that day — and that morning the person's bell reminds them. The reminder never carries the note's words and never goes by email.  HR can announce a meeting with (Employee, Branch, Full Company) in that calendar and will be notified to all or selected persons. 

|  |
| :-: |
| **WHAT AN RM SEES IN ADDITION**A licence panel that counts down in plain words: *"Valid. Expires 14 March 2027 — 6 months and 11 days remaining."*Four months before expiry the wording changes to **"Renewal due"**, turns amber, and a notification and email go out — to the RM, their manager, the HR Executive and the HR Head on the same day, so it does not depend on one person noticing. If the date passes, the status turns red and reads **"Expired"** and stays at the top of HR's register until it is resolved. **It never blocks the person's account or their work** — as you decided, the system warns and nothing more. |

### **5.2   Manager, Department Head, Divisional Manager**

All four employee pages — because a manager is also an employee who takes leave and has a file — plus one page about other people.

**Page 5 — Approvals and requests.** Three sections on one screen:

• **Leave waiting for me.** Every application from the manager's own team, showing who, what type, which dates, how many working days, the reason, and how much leave that person has left. Two buttons: **Grant** or **Deny**. A denial needs a written reason — the system will not accept an empty one. **Granting does not finish the application**; it passes it to the HR Head. See Section 7.

• **Raise a requisition.** One form with a type dropdown: office supplies, IT equipment, money or expense, or a request for new staff.

• **Submit the branch attendance sheet.** Once a month. The whole branch as a grid — people down the side, days across the top. Approved leave is filled in automatically and cannot be contradicted. On Submit the sheet locks and goes to HR.

**What a manager cannot see:** another branch, another department, another manager's team, or anybody's bank details and NID. If somebody transfers between teams, the old manager keeps access to the records of the period when that person reported to them, and loses access to everything after the transfer date.

### **5.3   HR Executive**

The four employee pages, plus four working pages. This is the person who does the daily HR work.

**Page 5 — Set up an employee.** Used at the moment of approving a new joiner, and whenever somebody's situation changes. Four jobs: issue the employee ID (the system proposes the next one automatically — see Section 12); put the person in a category or grade; set who they report to; set their branch. A transfer is recorded as a dated event, never by overwriting: the old branch keeps its history, the new branch takes over from the transfer date.

**Page 6 — Find anybody.** One searchable list of every employee, RM and manager, with filters down the side: branch, department, role, category, manager, joining date, and active or left. Type a name or an ID and the list narrows as you type. Results can be exported to a spreadsheet, and every export is recorded — a list of every employee's details leaving the building is exactly the kind of event an auditor asks about.

**Page 7 — The RM certificate register.** Every RM on one screen with issue date, expiry date, and a status that colours itself: green for valid, amber inside four months, red once expired. HR uploads renewed certificates here and enters the new dates, and the countdown restarts.

**Page 8 — Record a leaver.** The exit process described in Section 6.6. Both the HR Executive and the HR Head can record it.

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| **An HR Executive CAN** | **An HR Executive CANNOT** |
| Create accounts · check and approve documents · issue employee IDs · assign branches, categories and managers · keep the RM certificate register · record a leaver · view any staff file | Approve leave · approve requisitions · issue show-cause letters · create or close branches · manage trading terminals · see company-wide analysis reports |

  

The division is deliberate: the HR Executive handles **records**, the HR Head handles **decisions**. One person entering the data and a different person approving it is a basic control, and the sort of separation a regulator expects to see.

### **5.4   HR Head**

Everything the HR Executive has, plus five more pages. The widest panel in the system.  The HR Head's **own** leave goes straight up to the Super Admin — the HR Head cannot approve their own leave.

**Page 9 — Approvals.** One inbox holding everything waiting: leave applications passed up by managers, leave from managers and HR Executives, and requisitions from every department. Each item shows who raised it, when, what the level below already decided, and the full history. Approving a leave application passes it up to the Super Admin; denying it stops it there.

**Page 10 — Reports and analysis.** Described in Section 6.10.

**Page 11 — Compliance and show-cause letters.** Described in Section 6.5.

**Page 12 — Branches.** Create a branch, record its details — name, code, address, phone, opening date, branch manager — and see who is attached to it.

**Page 13 — Trading terminals (TWS).** The register of every Trader Work Station ID the company holds. Described in Section 6.9.

  

### **5.5   Super Admin**

Approval of the HR Head's account; **the final approval on every leave application in the company**; final approval of requisitions above a value FCSL sets; the ability to create and deactivate accounts of any kind; and read-only access to the permanent record of every action taken in the system.

It is a small panel used briefly but daily — the leave chain ends here, so the Super Admin's inbox must be cleared regularly or applications stall at the last step. That is a real operational commitment and is worth being aware of before go-live. The morning summary email in Section 8 exists mainly for this reason.

## **6. The ten things the system does**

Set aside who sees what, and the system is ten working parts. Six are used across the company; four are registers only HR touches. Underneath all ten sits one permanent record of who did what, and when.

### **6.1   Records and documents**

The foundation. Everything else points back to a person's record.

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| **Group** | **Contents** |
| **Identity** | Employee ID, full name, father's and mother's name, date of birth, photograph, gender, nationality, religion, marital status |
| **Employment** | Role, category or grade, branch, who they report to, joining date, confirmation date, and status — **Active** or **Left** |
| **Documents** | Every file, with who uploaded it, when, whether HR accepted it, and every earlier version if it was replaced |
| **Emergency** | Mandatory contact and alternate contact, with the history of every change |
| **Bank** | Account name, number, bank, branch, routing number |
| **Licence** (RM only) | RM certificate, number, issue date, expiry date, full renewal history |
| **History** | Transfers, promotions, changes of manager, resignation and last working day — each with a date, never overwritten |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **THE RULE ABOUT DELETING THINGS**A wrong document is **superseded**, not removed — the new version becomes current and the old one stays underneath, marked replaced, with the reason and the name of whoever replaced it. A person who leaves is **marked as Left** on a date; their record stays whole. A brokerage that cannot produce a former employee's file when asked has a real problem, and the cheapest way to guarantee it can is to make deletion impossible in the first place. The single exception is the one-year document rule in Section 12. |

**How documents are kept safe:** files sit in a private area with no public web address, so there is no link that works if somebody forwards it. Opening a document produces a temporary link that stops working after a few minutes. Every view is recorded — who looked at whose NID, and when. Files are encrypted where they are stored, so a copy of the raw storage is useless on its own.

### **6.2   Leave**

The part of the system used most often, by the most people, and therefore the part that has to be simplest. The approval route is in Section 7; this is about the form itself.

**Applying.** The employee picks a leave type, picks the dates on a calendar, and writes a reason. Before they press send the system already tells them three things: how many **working** days this costs — Fridays, Saturdays and public holidays are excluded automatically — how many days of that type they have left, and who the application is going to first.

|  |
| :-: |
| **WHAT THE SYSTEM CHECKS BEFORE ACCEPTING AN APPLICATION**• Are the dates in the future, or is this a late application that needs a reason?• Does the person have enough days of that type left?• Do these dates overlap an application they have already made?• Does the type require an attachment — a medical certificate for extended sick leave?• Are too many people in the same team already off on those dates? |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **DECIDED — HR SETS THE LEAVE TYPES, NOT THE PROGRAMMER**There is a settings screen where the HR Head adds leave types and the number of days each one carries, says whether unused days carry into the next year and up to what limit, and says whether an application over the balance is refused or merely warned about.It ships pre-filled with **FCSL's own figures** *(decided 10 September 2026)* — casual 6 days, sick 6 days, earned leave 20 days that do not carry forward, maternity 16 weeks — which FCSL can change or rename without needing a developer. Each change is saved with the date it takes effect, so last year's leave is still calculated on last year's rule and old records do not silently change meaning. |
| **DECIDED — THE LEAVE YEAR AND PROBATION** *(FCSL, 10 September 2026)* Each person's leave year runs from their own joining date. During probation — the first year, or until HR records a confirmation date — casual and sick leave can still be taken, up to the full 6 days each, but those days come out of the first permanent year. Somebody who takes 3 days of casual leave and 2 of sick while on probation has 3 casual and 4 sick in their first permanent year, and the full 6 and 6 the year after. Earned leave begins when probation ends. The HR Head sets, per leave type, which of these a type follows. |

**Withdrawing.** Plans change. An employee can withdraw an application that has not yet been fully approved, and can request cancellation of one already approved if the dates have not started. The days go back onto the balance and the record shows what happened.

### **6.3   Attendance**

Attendance flows in one direction, once a month, through the branch manager.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **When** | **Who** | **What happens** |
| Through the month | Branch manager | Fills the grid as days pass, or all at once at month end. Approved leave, Fridays, Saturdays and public holidays are already filled in and cannot be contradicted. |
| First working days of the next month | Branch manager | Reviews and presses Submit. The sheet locks. If a branch has not submitted by the deadline, HR sees it on a list of missing branches. |
| After that | HR | Checks the sheets against leave records, queries anything odd, corrects with a reason recorded, then publishes the month. |
| Once published | Employee | Sees their own month as a calendar. Before publication it reads "Not yet published". |

  
  

**What can be marked:** Present · Absent · Late · On approved leave · Public holiday · Weekly off (Friday and Saturday) · On official duty outside the branch. 

**Why the sheet locks on submission.** An attendance record that can be changed quietly afterwards is worth nothing in a dispute. Once submitted, only HR can change it, only with a reason, and the change stays visible for ever alongside the original entry. The manager can see what HR changed and why.

### **6.4   Requisition**

One form, four types. The form changes to suit the type chosen, but the journey afterwards is the same for all of them, which is why it is one feature and not four.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **Type** | **Extra fields it asks for** | **Ends with** |
| **Office supplies** | Item, quantity, when needed | Admin issues it and marks it delivered |
| **IT equipment** | Item, specification, replacement or additional | IT issues it; the item is recorded against the person |
| **Money / expense** | Amount, purpose, advance or reimbursement, bill attached | Accounts pays and marks it settled |
| **New staff** | Position, how many, why, when needed, budget | HR opens recruitment outside this system |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **DECIDED — WHO MAY RAISE ONE****Managers, department heads and the HR Head only** *(amended by FCSL, 10 September 2026)*. Employees, RMs and HR Executives cannot raise a requisition — they ask their manager, who raises it on their behalf. The requisition then goes from the manager to the HR Head, and above a value FCSL sets, to the Super Admin. The HR Head's own requisition goes straight to the Super Admin. The requester watches it move through those steps on their own screen and is told the moment it is decided, which removes the follow-up phone calls that make up most of the delay in a paper process. |

### **6.5   Compliance and show-cause letters**

The disciplinary process, carried out inside the software instead of on paper. Here is what that means in practice:

**1.** The HR Head opens a show-cause against a named employee and writes the letter, choosing from approved templates so the wording and legal footing stay consistent.

**2.** The employee receives it — a notification, an email, and a red banner across their panel that does not go away.

**3.** The employee writes a reply inside the system and may attach evidence.  The system **produces that PDF automatically** from what they write, so nothing has to be typed in Word and scanned.

**4.** The HR Head reads the reply and records the outcome: closed with no action, warning issued, or escalated. Letter, reply and outcome are stored together in that person's file permanently.

|  |
| :-: |
| **THIS NEEDS STRICTER PRIVACY THAN ANY OTHER PAGE**A show-cause is visible to the HR Head, the employee concerned, and nobody else by default — not even their manager, unless the HR Head chooses to include them. Every single time this record is opened, the system records who opened it. What this replaces is a printed letter, a signature on a receipt copy, a typed reply and a photocopy in a file, with no reliable way to prove afterwards when the letter was actually received. Timestamped delivery and receipt protects the employee and the company equally. |

### **6.6   Exit and clearance** 

Release letters and clearance documents but has no page for handling somebody's departure. That page now exists, because an employee leaving is one of the highest-risk moments in a brokerage — their trading terminal must be surrendered, their certificate status changed, and their system access closed the same day.

**Who records it:** the HR Head and HR Executives, both.

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| **Step** | **What the system does** |
| **1. Record the departure** | HR enters the reason — resignation, end of contract, termination, retirement — and the **last working day**. |
| **2. Clearance checklist** | A list of what must be returned and cleared: IT equipment, ID card, keys, any outstanding advance from Accounts, handover of client files. Each line is ticked off by the department responsible. |
| **3. Release the licence and terminal** | The RM certificate is marked surrendered and the person drops out of the expiry register. Any TWS terminal held is marked surrendered and freed for reassignment. **The system will not let HR finish an exit while a terminal is still assigned** — the single most important safeguard in the module. |
| **4. Access closes automatically** | At the end of the last working day the account stops working by itself. Nobody has to remember. |
| **5. Release letter** | Produced from a template and stored in the person's file. |
| **6. Mark the ID as Left** | The employee ID is marked **Left**, with the date. The ID is never reused and never deleted. The record stays whole so their history remains readable. |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **WHAT MARKING SOMEBODY "LEFT" MAKES POSSIBLE**This is the point of the whole module. Because every person carries an Active or Left status with a date, the system can answer instantly, and correctly, at any moment in time:**Total active people**, split into employees, RMs and managers · **total who have left**, over any period · the same figures **per branch** and **per department** · how many joined and how many left this month, this quarter, this year · and the **net change in headcount**. Section 6.10 turns these into the report the HR Head sees. |

### **6.7   Branches**

Create a branch and record its details — name, code, address, phone, opening date, branch manager. See everybody attached to it: managers, employees and RMs. Move people between branches as a dated transfer, never by overwriting. Closing a branch is marking it closed on a date, and its people must be moved first. The history survives.

### **6.8   The RM certificate register**

Every RM in the company on one screen, with issue date, expiry date, and a status that colours itself green, amber or red. Sorted so the most urgent sits at the top. HR uploads renewed certificates and enters the new dates, and the countdown restarts. This single page is the answer to the first problem in Section 1.

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **WHY FOUR MONTHS, AND WHY IT ONLY WARNS****Four months** because renewal is not instant — the paperwork has to be prepared, signed, submitted and processed by the regulator. Four months gives room for all of that plus one round of something going wrong. A one-month warning would arrive too late to be useful.**Warning only, never blocking** — as FCSL decided. An expired certificate turns red and stays at the top of the register until resolved, but the person's account keeps working normally and nothing is taken away from them. Blocking somebody's work over a date typed in wrongly would cause more damage than it prevents. |

### **6.9   Trading terminals (TWS)**

A register of every Trader Work Station ID the company holds and who is currently using it. For each terminal: the terminal ID, which exchange, the branch it sits in, the person it is assigned to, the date it was assigned, and its status — **active, suspended or surrendered**.

|  |
| :-: |
| **WHY THIS BELONGS IN THE HR SYSTEM AND NOT WITH IT**A trading terminal is tied to a person, and that person's right to use it depends on their certificate being valid and on their still being employed. Keeping the terminal register next to the certificate register and the staff list means the system can answer the awkward question by itself: **is anybody holding an active terminal whose certificate has expired, or who has already left the company?** That question is very hard to answer when the two lists live in different departments — and it is exactly the question a BSEC inspection asks. |

### **6.10   Reports and analysis**

In practice, a dashboard answering the questions a Head of HR is actually asked:

• **Headcount** — total active, split into employees, RMs and managers; the same per branch and per department; who joined and who left this month; and the net change. This is the report the exit process in 6.6 exists to make possible.

• **Attendance** — the branches with the most absence, the most lateness, the lowest attendance.

• **Leave** — days taken by type; who is close to exhausting their entitlement; and who has taken none at all, which is itself worth knowing in a brokerage.

• **Documents** — which staff files are still incomplete, and how long they have been that way.

• **Certificates** — how many RM certificates expire in the next three, six and twelve months.

• **Terminals** — how many TWS IDs are held, assigned and free, and any held by somebody whose certificate has expired or who has left.

Every report can be filtered by date range and branch, and exported to a spreadsheet.

### **Underneath all ten — the permanent record**

This part has no screen of its own and most people will never think about it. It is the reason the system can be trusted. Every action that changes something, or reveals somebody's private information, writes one line that can never be edited or deleted — not by HR, not by the Super Admin, not by the person who built the software.

|  |
| :-: |
| **WHAT A LINE LOOKS LIKE, IN PLAIN WORDS***"Rahim Uddin (HR Executive) approved the documents of Karim Hossain at 11:42 on 14 March 2026."**"Nasreen Akter (HR Head) opened the NID of Salma Begum at 09:15 on 2 April 2026."**"Employee A 288 - 19 - 70 marked Left on 30 April 2026 by Rahim Uddin — terminal DSE-4471 surrendered."* |

**What gets recorded:** every approval and refusal with its reason · every document uploaded, replaced or viewed · every change to personal, bank or employment details, with the old value and the new · every login and failed login attempt · every export of employee data. Only the Super Admin can read it *(amended by FCSL, 10 September 2026 — previously the HR Head as well)*, and they can only read it.

## **7. How an approval travels**

This section was the biggest gap in version 1.0 and is now fully settled. Read it carefully — it is the part most likely to cause an argument later if anybody has understood it differently.

### **7.1   The leave chain**

**Every leave application in the company ends at the Super Admin.** How many steps it takes to get there depends on who applied.

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **Who applies** | **Step 1** | **Step 2** | **Step 3 — final** |
| **Employee / RM** | Their **Manager** | **HR Head** | **Super Admin** |
| **Manager** | **HR Head** | **Super Admin** | — |
| **HR Executive** | **HR Head** | **Super Admin** | — |
| **HR Head** | **Super Admin** | — | — |
| **Super Admin** | No approval needed. Recorded directly. |  |  |

#### **The three rules that govern it**

|  |
| :-: |
| **RULE 1 — ONE STEP AT A TIME, IN ORDER**The application appears in only one person's inbox at a time. **When a step approves, the next person in the chain is notified and it appears in their inbox.** The HR Head never sees an application the manager has not yet dealt with, and the Super Admin never sees one the HR Head has not yet dealt with. Nobody can skip a step and nobody can approve out of turn. |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **RULE 2 — A DENIAL STOPS THE CHAIN DEAD**If the manager denies an employee's leave, **it is not passed to the HR Head and the Super Admin never hears about it.** The application is closed there. The same applies at every level: whoever denies it ends it.The applicant is told immediately, with the written reason, and can see exactly who denied it and when. Nobody above that point is notified, because as far as the chain is concerned the request no longer exists. A denial always requires a written reason — the system will not accept an empty one. |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **RULE 3 — WHEN THE SUPER ADMIN APPROVES, EVERYONE INVOLVED IS TOLD**The final approval is the only moment the leave is actually granted. At that point the system notifies **the applicant** and **every person who approved it along the way**. So when a Super Admin approves an employee's leave, the employee, their manager and the HR Head all receive the confirmation at the same time. The days come off the balance at this moment and not before. |

#### **A worked example — an employee asks for three days**

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **Moment** | **What happens** | **Who is notified** |
| Monday 10:00 | Karim applies for 3 days casual leave. | His manager. Karim's own screen shows *"Waiting with your manager"*. |
| Monday 15:20 | The manager presses **Grant**. | The HR Head. Karim's screen changes to *"Approved by manager — waiting with HR Head"*. |
| Tuesday 11:05 | The HR Head presses **Grant**. | The Super Admin. Karim's screen: *"Waiting with Super Admin"*. |
| **Tuesday 16:40** | **The Super Admin presses Grant. The leave is now granted.** | **Karim, his manager, and the HR Head — all three at once.** The 3 days come off Karim's balance. |

Had the manager pressed **Deny** on Monday afternoon, the story would have ended there: Karim told, with the reason; the HR Head and Super Admin never involved.

### **7.2   The other three chains**

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| **What** | **The route** |
| **A new person's documents** | The person uploads → **HR Executive or HR Head** approves. Done. The exception is the HR Head's own documents, which go to the Super Admin. |
| **Requisition** | Raised by a manager, department head or the HR Head → **HR Head** → **Super Admin**, but only if it is above the value FCSL sets. Below that value the HR Head's approval is final. The HR Head's own requisition goes straight to the **Super Admin**. Employees, RMs and HR Executives cannot raise one. |
| **Branch attendance sheet** | Branch manager submits → **HR verifies and corrects** → HR publishes → every employee in that branch can see their own month. |

### **7.3   What happens when nobody responds**

Every request that has waited more than **three working days** moves to the top of that person's inbox and turns amber, and a reminder email goes out. After a week, the person's own superior is told as well. **The system never silently approves anything on its own** — a request left alone stays open and becomes progressively harder to ignore. This matters most at the last step: because every leave application in the company ends at the Super Admin, that inbox has to be cleared regularly or applications will stall one step from the finish.

  

## **8. The alerts the system sends**

Two channels: a **bell icon inside the software** with a count of unread items, and an **email**. The bell is the complete list; email carries only what should interrupt somebody's day.

|  |  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **What happened** | **Who is told** | **Email** | **When** |
| **RM certificate expiring** | The RM, their manager, HR Executive, HR Head | **Yes** | 4 months before, then monthly, then weekly in the final month |
| **RM certificate expired** | The RM, HR Head, Super Admin | **Yes** | On the day, then weekly until resolved |
| Documents approved | The person | **Yes** | Immediately |
| Documents sent back | The person | **Yes** | Immediately, with the reason |
| New joiner waiting for review | HR Executive, HR Head | — | Immediately; reminder after 2 working days |
| **Leave reaches your step** | The next approver in the chain, one at a time | **Yes** | The moment the step below approves |
| **Leave finally granted** | The applicant **and every person who approved it** | **Yes** | The moment the Super Admin approves |
| **Leave denied** | The applicant only. Nobody above the person who denied it. | **Yes** | Immediately, with the reason |
| Requisition decided | The requester | **Yes** | Immediately |
| Attendance sheet due | Every branch manager | **Yes** | Last working day of the month, then daily if late |
| Branch has not submitted | HR Head | **Yes** | Three working days after the deadline |
| Monthly attendance published | Everyone in that branch | — | On publication |
| **Show-cause letter issued** | The employee only | **Yes** | Immediately, plus a banner that cannot be dismissed |
| Show-cause reply submitted | HR Head | **Yes** | Immediately |
| **Exit recorded / last day near** | HR Head, the person's manager, IT and Accounts for clearance | **Yes** | On recording, then 3 days before the last working day |
| **Terminal still assigned to a leaver** | HR Head, Super Admin | **Yes** | Daily until it is surrendered |
| Anything waiting over 3 days | The approver | **Yes** | Once daily, in a single morning summary |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **ONE RULE THAT KEEPS THIS USEFUL**Approvers get **one summary email each morning**, not one email per request. A system that sends fifteen emails before lunch trains people to ignore it, and then the one email that mattered — a certificate expiring — gets ignored along with the rest. This matters most for the Super Admin, who is the last step on every leave application in the company. |

**What the system will never do.** It will not send anybody's NID, bank details or documents by email. Notifications say what happened and carry a link back into the system, where the reader has to be logged in. Personal information never travels through an inbox.

## **12. The rules and numbers now fixed**

Everything on this page is settled and will be built exactly as stated. This is the page to check first if something later looks wrong.

### **12.1   The employee ID**

The format is **A XXX - YY - 70**, made of four parts:

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| **Part** | **What it means** |
| **A** | The letter block. Stays **A** until the running number reaches 999, then becomes **B** and the number starts again at 001. After B 999 it becomes C, and so on. Each letter block holds 999 people. |
| **XXX** | The running number, always three digits with leading zeros. **It never resets at the year end and no number is ever reused**, including after somebody leaves. |
| **YY** | The last two digits of the **joining year**. Somebody joining in 2026 gets 26; in 2027, 27. This never changes for that person afterwards. |
| **70** | Fixed. Always 70, for everybody, for ever. |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **WORKED THROUGH, USING YOUR REAL NUMBERS**Your highest existing ID is **A 412 - 26 - 70**. So:• The next person to join, in 2026  →  **A 413 - 26 - 70**• The one after that, also 2026  →  **A 414 - 26 - 70**• The first person to join in 2027  →  **A 415 - 27 - 70**  *(the number carries on, only the year changes)*• Eventually, after A 999 - YY - 70  →  **B 001 - YY - 70**The system proposes the next ID automatically at the moment HR approves a joiner's documents. HR can override it if there is a reason, but the system will refuse an ID that has already been used by anybody, ever. |

  

|  |
| :-: |
| **THE 412 EXISTING STAFF**412 existing employees data import after the portal is finished, keeping the IDs they already have. The system is built for this: there is a bulk import screen that takes a spreadsheet, checks every row before saving anything, and reports exactly which rows have a problem and why. Existing staff are created directly at Stage 2 — they do not go through the locked door, because they are already employed and their files already exist. HR then attaches their documents over time. The running counter is set to start at 413 once the import is done. |

### **12.2   Everything else that is now fixed**

|  |  |
| :-: | :-: |
| **Rule** | **What was decided** |
| **Weekly off** | **Friday and Saturday.** Both are excluded automatically when counting leave days, and are pre-filled on the attendance grid. |
| **Public holidays** | The HR Head enters the year's holiday calendar once, at the start of each year, on a settings screen. Government holidays in Bangladesh are announced annually, so this cannot be built into the software permanently. |
| **Leave types and days** | Set by the HR Head on a settings screen, pre-filled with Bangladesh Labour Act 2006 minimums. Changing them needs no developer. Each change is dated, so old leave records keep their original meaning. |
| **Leave approval** | Employee → Manager → HR Head → Super Admin, in that order. A denial at any step ends the application and nobody above is told. Full rules in Section 7. |
| **RM certificate warning** | **Four months before expiry.** Warning only — **the system never blocks the person or their work**, whatever the certificate status. |
| **Document retention** | **One year** after the last working day, then the uploaded files are removed automatically. **The employee record itself is kept permanently** — name, ID, dates, role, branch history, and the fact that they left — so the headcount and joiner-leaver reports stay correct for ever. Only the scanned files go. |
| **Requisitions** | Managers, HR and Super Admin only. Employees and RMs ask their manager. |
| **Attendance** | Branch manager submits monthly, HR verifies and publishes. No punch machines, no self check-in. |
| **Reading documents** | The system reads NIDs and certificates and fills the form, but every field stays marked unconfirmed until a human in HR agrees with it. |
| **Language** | English only. |
| **Hosting** | Cloud server. See the note below before a region is chosen. |
| **Alerts** | In-app bell plus email. No SMS. |

  
  
  

## **13. How it would be built, and in what order**

Four phases. Each ends with something FCSL can actually use, rather than everything arriving at once at the end. If the project stopped after any phase, what had been delivered would still be worth having.

|  |  |  |
| :-: | :-: | :-: |
| **Phase** | **What gets built** | **What FCSL can do at the end of it** |
| **1 — Accounts, documents and the locked door** | Logging in, the five panels, the upload screen, reading NID and certificates with HR confirming, HR's review queue, employee IDs, branches, **the bulk import of your 412 existing staff**, and the permanent record underneath it all. | Onboard every existing member of staff and finally have one complete, searchable set of employee files. Solves two of the six problems in Section 1 on its own. |
| **2 — Leave and attendance** | The leave settings screen, the holiday calendar, applying, the three-step approval chain, the branch attendance grid, HR verification and monthly publication. | Paper leave forms stop. This is the phase every employee will notice. |
| **3 — Requisition, compliance and exit** | The requisition form and its approval route, show-cause letters, employee replies as automatic PDFs, and the full exit and clearance process. | The internal paper trail moves fully inside the system, and leavers stop being invisible. |
| **4 — The registers and the reports** | The RM certificate register with its four-month warning, the trading terminal register, and the HR Head's dashboard and exports. | Nobody ever again discovers a certificate expiry by accident, and the real headcount is one click away. |

### **What it will be built with, in one paragraph**

A single website, working equally on a desktop in the office and on a phone in a branch. One database holding everything, with the privacy rules from Section 9 enforced inside the database itself rather than only on the screen. Documents in private encrypted storage with links that expire. Reading NIDs and certificates handled by an artificial-intelligence service, with every field it produces marked unconfirmed until a human agrees. Email for the alerts. Nightly automatic backups. Nothing exotic — these are ordinary, well-proven choices, deliberately, because an HR system needs to still be running in five years.
